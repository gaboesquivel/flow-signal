Decide by reading side by side. Your words sit next to the need you might join, and a PM reviewing a merge sees the same two-column view.

Principles
Each screen has one primary action. Lists are ruled one-line rows, not cards. Only the thing being decided gets space: the selected match in submit, the one expanded row in triage and in needs. The side-by-side reading is the only rich layout, and customers and PMs both use it, so the product has one way of saying "are these the same need?". Ledger's pencil/ink rule is kept as is. AI text sits behind a dashed rule in graphite-indigo, approved text behind a solid rule in ink, and customer words appear as heavy-ruled quotes.
Risks
One-line match rows cut statements short, so people must click a row to read it. Some will only read the pre-selected first match. On phone the columns stack and the side-by-side benefit is mostly lost; it becomes "read the need, then your words". In triage, collapsed rows hide the AI text and its pencil state. That speeds PMs up, but can invite accepting without reading. The needs table is desktop-first.
Not shown
No comments or attachments (MVP). Theme filter is dropped from the public board on purpose; search plus status is enough for six needs.
