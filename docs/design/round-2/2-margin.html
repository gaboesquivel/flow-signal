Signal · Design round 2 · Variant 2

Margin

Your words stay in view. A narrow left margin carries the steps and the person's own entry, as written, so every match is read against what they actually said.

Principles
One column of work, one primary action per screen. The margin replaces labels and progress chrome: three plain step words and the entry itself. No cards, no pills, rules only where a list needs them. Writing is welcome: the entry is shown back in full, in ink, with an Edit link, and the AI's restatement sits under it in pencil so the difference between "what I said" and "what the AI heard" is visible at a glance. The console uses the same move: a quiet list on the left, the full context of one decision on the right.
Pencil / ink
Unchanged from Ledger: AI text sits behind a dashed rule with an italic small-caps label in --ai-foreground; confirmed text is ink. PM-approved summaries and decision notes are ink; "why it matches", the restatement, drafted statements and unconfirmed scores are pencil.
Risks
The margin costs 280px; on mid-size laptops the match text column gets narrower than ideal. On phone the entry collapses to one line, so the "read against your own words" benefit is weaker exactly where screens are smallest. A long body could make the margin taller than the step. In triage, master–detail hides the rest of the queue's content: PMs must click through rather than scan everything at once.
